Preamble

The Controller uses the Fratius service under Fratius's General Terms and Conditions (the “Main Contract”). In doing so, Fratius processes personal data on behalf of the Controller. This agreement governs the parties' rights and obligations under Art. 28 GDPR. It is concluded electronically together with the Main Contract (Art. 28(9) GDPR). In case of conflict, this agreement takes precedence over the Main Contract in data protection matters.

§ 1 Subject matter, duration, nature and purpose of the processing

(1) Subject matter: Fratius provides the Controller with the Fratius fleet software (web application, iPhone and Android apps) as software-as-a-service and in doing so processes the data that the Controller and its Users enter, upload or generate with the software. This includes hosting, storage, AI analysis of photos, sending push notifications, data export and support.

(2) Duration: this agreement applies for the term of the Main Contract including the free trial and a transitional and retrieval period under § 15 of the Main Contract. The obligations under this agreement continue until Fratius has deleted or returned all of the Controller's data.

(3) Nature of the processing (Art. 4(2) GDPR): collection via the apps and the web application, storage, organisation, reading photos with AI, retrieval, display, transfer to the sub-processors under Annex 3, export, restriction and erasure.

(4) Purpose of the processing: documentation of vehicle handover and vehicle condition, review of possible damage by the Controller, history of vehicle use, tours, refuelling and washing, driver and vehicle records, incidents, deadlines, checks and notifications, in each case for the Controller's fleet management.

(5) Type of personal data and categories of data subjects: see Annex 1.

(6) Place of processing: the data is stored and processed by the sub-processors under Annex 3, mainly in the USA (Google Cloud, region us-central1 and multi-region nam5). The AI analysis at Anthropic takes place in the USA and, where Anthropic uses them, in other countries. The Controller hereby instructs Fratius to process the data in these countries as described in Annex 3. § 6 applies to the transfer.

(7) This agreement does not cover processing for which Fratius itself is controller, in particular user accounts (login), sign-up, contract handling and billing with the Controller, and technical logs for the security of the service. Fratius's privacy policies (fratius.com/datenschutz and fratius.com/app-privacy) apply to that processing.

§ 2 Instructions

(1) Fratius processes the data only on documented instructions from the Controller (Art. 28(3) sentence 2(a) GDPR). Instructions are the Main Contract, this agreement and the use and configuration of the software by the Controller and its Users (e.g. switching on the tours function, approving a driver, marking damage). The Controller gives further instructions in text form to info@fratius.com.

(2) On the Controller's side, the fleet owner and any further person the Controller names to Fratius in text form are authorised to give instructions. The recipient of instructions at Fratius is Casian Dan Colto (info@fratius.com).

(3) The instructions include the deletion rules set in the software: photos and videos of a month are deleted on the 15th of the following month (retention 15 to 45 days), but not while the vehicle is still with the driver who took it over; they are then deleted on the 15th of the month after the vehicle was returned. Photos and videos marked as damage are deleted on the 15th following the 90th day after they were taken. Photos of fuel cards and documents are deleted right after reading; only the last four digits of the fuel card are stored. Original videos are deleted once the playback copy has been created. Handovers, walk-arounds, damage checks, checks, tours, refuelling and washing, driver and vehicle records and incidents remain stored as an ongoing history for the term of the Main Contract; Users cannot delete them in the software. If the Controller requests the correction or deletion of individual entries that it cannot change itself in the software (e.g. to fulfil a data subject's right under Art. 16 or 17 GDPR), Fratius carries it out on instruction in text form.

(4) Fratius treats instructions that go beyond the agreed scope of services as a request to change the services.

(5) If Fratius considers that an instruction infringes the GDPR or other data protection provisions, Fratius informs the Controller without undue delay (Art. 28(3) sentence 3 GDPR). Fratius may suspend carrying out the instruction until the Controller confirms or changes it.

(6) If Fratius is required by Union or Member State law to carry out other processing, Fratius informs the Controller of that legal requirement before processing, unless that law prohibits such information (Art. 28(3) sentence 2(a) GDPR).

§ 3 Confidentiality

(1) Fratius grants access to the data only to persons who strictly need it to perform the contract. Fratius ensures that these persons have committed themselves to confidentiality or are under an appropriate statutory obligation of confidentiality (Art. 28(3) sentence 2(b), Art. 29, Art. 32(4) GDPR).

(2) Currently only the owner of Fratius has access. If Fratius uses employees or freelancers in future, Fratius obliges them to confidentiality in writing or electronically before they start and instructs them in data protection.

(3) Fratius accesses the Controller's data only to the extent necessary for operation, troubleshooting, support or on the Controller's instructions.

§ 4 Security of processing

(1) Fratius takes the technical and organisational measures under Art. 32 GDPR described in Annex 2. The Controller has reviewed these measures and considers them appropriate to the risks of the processing.

(2) The measures are subject to technical progress. Fratius may replace them with equivalent or better measures; the level of protection must not decrease. Fratius documents material changes and notifies the Controller of them.

§ 5 Sub-processors

(1) The Controller gives Fratius general authorisation to engage sub-processors (Art. 28(2) GDPR). The sub-processors engaged at the conclusion of the contract and listed in Annex 3 are deemed approved.

(2) Fratius informs the Controller in text form (e.g. by email to the fleet owner) at least 30 days in advance of any intended addition or replacement of a sub-processor, stating name, seat, service and place of processing. The Controller may object in text form within 14 days of receiving the information for an important data protection reason. If the parties do not find a solution, the Controller may terminate the Main Contract extraordinarily with effect from the date of the change. If an immediate change is necessary to avert a risk to the data or because a service is discontinued, Fratius informs the Controller as early as possible.

(3) Fratius contractually binds each sub-processor to essentially the same data protection obligations as set out in this agreement, in particular to sufficient guarantees for appropriate technical and organisational measures (Art. 28(4) GDPR). For the large providers listed in Annex 3, this is done through their standard contract terms, which Fratius cannot negotiate; Annex 3 states the contractual basis for each. Fratius remains responsible to the Controller for the sub-processors' compliance with their obligations (Art. 28(4) sentence 2 GDPR).

(4) On request, Fratius provides the Controller with the sub-processors' data processing terms (or where to find them) and, where available, their lists of further sub-processors.

(5) Ancillary services where the service provider has no access to the Controller's data (e.g. telecommunications, post) are not sub-processing. The payment service provider Stripe processes only the Controller's contract and payment data, for which Fratius itself and, for its own legal duties, Stripe is controller; Stripe receives no data that Fratius processes on the Controller's behalf and is therefore not a sub-processor under this agreement.

§ 6 Transfers to third countries

(1) Fratius transfers data to a third country only on documented instructions from the Controller and only under the conditions of Chapter V GDPR (Art. 44 et seq.). The Controller gives the instruction for the transfers listed in Annex 3 with this agreement.

(2) Transfers to sub-processors in the USA are based on the European Commission's adequacy decision of 10 July 2023 (Art. 45 GDPR) to the extent the recipient is certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. Otherwise, including for transfers to other third countries, they are based on the European Commission's standard contractual clauses (Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914, in particular Module 3 – processor to processor), which are contained in the sub-processors' contract terms (Art. 46(2)(c) GDPR).

(3) If a transfer mechanism becomes invalid or inapplicable (e.g. through a decision of the Court of Justice of the EU in the pending appeal C-703/25 P on the Data Privacy Framework), Fratius informs the Controller without undue delay and switches the transfer to another permissible mechanism. If that is not possible, either party may terminate the Main Contract extraordinarily.

§ 7 Assistance to the Controller

(1) Data subject rights: Fratius assists the Controller with appropriate technical and organisational measures in fulfilling data subjects' rights under Chapter III GDPR (Art. 28(3) sentence 2(e) GDPR). The Controller can display, partly correct and export much of the data itself in the software (§ 15 of the Main Contract); entries it cannot change or delete in the software are corrected or deleted by Fratius on its instructions (§ 2(3)). If a data subject contacts Fratius directly, Fratius forwards the request to the Controller without undue delay and does not answer it itself unless the Controller instructs Fratius to do so.

(2) Obligations under Art. 32 to 36 GDPR: taking into account the nature of processing and the information available to Fratius, Fratius assists the Controller with the security of processing, with notifying personal data breaches to the supervisory authority and communicating them to data subjects, with a data protection impact assessment and with a prior consultation of the supervisory authority (Art. 28(3) sentence 2(f) GDPR). In particular, Fratius provides the information about the service, the sub-processors and the measures under Annex 2 needed for a data protection impact assessment.

§ 8 Notification of personal data breaches

(1) Fratius notifies the Controller of a personal data breach affecting the Controller's data without undue delay after becoming aware of it (Art. 33(2) GDPR).

(2) The notification contains, as far as known: a description of the nature of the breach, if possible with the categories and approximate number of data subjects and records concerned; the name and contact details of a contact point; the likely consequences; the measures taken or proposed to address and mitigate it. If Fratius cannot provide all information at the same time, it provides it in phases without further undue delay.

(3) Fratius takes the necessary measures without undue delay to secure the data and to mitigate possible adverse effects for the data subjects, and coordinates this with the Controller.

(4) Fratius also informs the Controller without undue delay of inspections and measures by a supervisory authority insofar as they concern this processing, and if the data at Fratius is endangered by attachment, seizure, insolvency proceedings or other events caused by third parties.

(5) Notifying the supervisory authority (Art. 33 GDPR) and communicating to data subjects (Art. 34 GDPR) is the Controller's responsibility.

§ 9 Deletion and return at the end of the contract

(1) After the end of the Main Contract, the Controller may, at its choice, request return of the data by export (§ 15 of the Main Contract) or waive the export. After the retrieval period under § 15(3) of the Main Contract has expired, Fratius deletes all of the Controller's personal data including existing copies, unless Union or Member State law requires storage (Art. 28(3) sentence 2(g) GDPR). This agreement continues to apply until deletion or return.

(2) At the sub-processors, deletion follows their terms (at Google Cloud and Firebase, after a recovery period of up to 30 days, within at most 180 days; at Anthropic, according to Anthropic, inputs and outputs are generally deleted within 30 days).

(3) On request, Fratius confirms deletion in text form, stating the date.

(4) During the term, Fratius deletes data under the deletion rules in § 2(3) and on the Controller's instructions.

§ 10 Evidence and audits

(1) Fratius makes available to the Controller all information necessary to demonstrate compliance with the obligations under Art. 28 GDPR and this agreement (Art. 28(3) sentence 2(h) GDPR), in particular the current description of the measures under Annex 2, the list of sub-processors and the sub-processors' evidence (e.g. their certificates and audit reports, to the extent they make them available).

(2) The Controller or an auditor mandated by it who is bound to confidentiality and is not a competitor of Fratius may verify compliance with this agreement, including by inspections. Audits must be announced with reasonable notice (usually at least 14 days), take place during normal business hours and must not disrupt operations disproportionately. They usually take place at most once per calendar year, unless there are indications of a breach or a supervisory authority requires an audit. Each party bears its own costs.

(3) The data centres and systems of the sub-processors (Google, Anthropic, Expo) are verified through their certificates and audit reports; Fratius cannot arrange on-site audits at these providers.

§ 11 Obligations of the Controller

(1) The Controller is responsible for the lawfulness of the processing, in particular for the legal basis, informing data subjects, involving a works council and any required data protection impact assessment (see § 9(3) of the Main Contract).

(2) The Controller informs Fratius without undue delay if it detects errors or irregularities in the processing.

(3) The Controller does not enter special categories of personal data (Art. 9 GDPR) or data on criminal offences (Art. 10 GDPR) into the service unless there is a legal basis for this.

§ 12 Liability

(1) Art. 82 GDPR applies to liability towards data subjects.

(2) Between the parties, Art. 82(4) and (5) GDPR apply. Otherwise, the liability rules of the Main Contract (§ 13 of the terms) apply, unless mandatory law provides otherwise.

Annex 1 – Categories of data subjects and types of personal data

1. Categories of data subjects

  • the Controller's employees (drivers, fleet managers, fleet owners), including marginal employees, temporary staff and agency workers;
  • drivers of the Controller's subcontractors, to the extent the Controller admits them;
  • persons who sign up with the Controller's driver link and are waiting for approval or are rejected;
  • drivers' emergency contacts;
  • persons involved in incidents (e.g. parties to accidents, complainants);
  • contact persons of the Controller's contractual partners (e.g. lessors, insurers, workshops), to the extent they appear in vehicle records or documents;
  • uninvolved persons who happen to be visible or audible in photos or videos (e.g. passers-by, number plates of other vehicles).

2. Types of personal data

  • user data within the Controller's company: name, mobile number, Transporter ID (for drivers), role, station;
  • sign-up requests through the driver link: name, email address, mobile number and Transporter ID (where given), the station picked, time and decision;
  • handovers: time, persons involved, vehicle, mileage, photos and videos of the vehicle (videos in the web application possibly with sound), damage markings, AI results, fleet managers' decisions;
  • tours (only if switched on): dashboard photos, mileage, fuel level, warning lights, assignment to driver and vehicle;
  • refuelling and washing: time, driver, vehicle, quantity, amount, last four digits of the fuel card, receipt photo; the photo of the fuel card is deleted after reading;
  • driver records: driving licence classes and their expiry dates, dates and results of licence checks, type and period of employment, briefings, emergency contact (name, phone number), notes;
  • incidents: accidents, traffic fines, complaints with related details and assignment to driver and vehicle;
  • vehicle records: registration, contracts, insurance, costs (mostly not personal data, but may be linked to persons);
  • document photos: read with AI and then deleted; the extracted details are stored;
  • checks (e.g. oil, tyres): time, person, result;
  • push notifications: content of the notification, device identifier for push (push token or the browser's push address), device type.

By design of the service, the following are not collected: copies or numbers of driving licences, date of birth, home address, health data and location data; videos are stored without location information. Special categories of personal data (Art. 9 GDPR) are not intended; they may, however, be contained in free-text fields or photos. Details of traffic fines may be data on criminal or administrative offences within the meaning of Art. 10 GDPR.

Annex 2 – Technical and organisational measures (Art. 32 GDPR)

1. General

Fratius does not operate its own servers. The service runs entirely on Google Cloud and Firebase (Firestore, Cloud Storage, Cloud Functions, Hosting, Authentication) in the USA (region us-central1, multi-region nam5). Physical security of the data centres lies with Google. Google states that the Firebase services are ISO 27001 certified and have undergone SOC 1, SOC 2 and SOC 3 audits. These are Google's evidence, not certifications of Fratius. Fratius itself is not certified.

2. Confidentiality (Art. 32(1)(b) GDPR)

Physical access control: data centres: by Google (see no. 1). Fratius does not operate its own servers or data centres.

System access control:

  • Sign-in via Firebase Authentication; Firebase stores passwords only as hashes, which Fratius cannot read.
  • Drivers join only through the Controller's driver link and get access only after the fleet owner or a fleet manager of the station they picked approves them; until then they see no data of the company. The fleet owner can create a new driver link; the previous one then stops working at once.
  • Fleet managers join only through invitation links that can be used only once and expire after 24 hours.
  • API keys (e.g. Anthropic) are held only server-side in Google Secret Manager and used only by the Cloud Functions; they are neither in the apps nor in the source code.

Data access control:

  • Role model: fleet owner (whole company), fleet manager (one station and its drivers), driver (own functions only). Only owners see costs in vehicle records.
  • The role model is enforced server-side (Firestore Security Rules and Cloud Functions), not only in the app's user interface, and tested automatically before every new version is released.
  • Access by Fratius (“app creator” role with an overall view across all customers): only for operation, troubleshooting, support or on instruction.

Separation control: each customer's data is assigned to one company and is not visible to users of other customers. All data of a customer is stored under its company; the Firestore Security Rules allow access only to members of that company (tested automatically). Automated tests run on local emulators with a separate test project, never with live data. Demo companies contain only made-up data and are marked as demo; they are in the same Firebase project as live data.

Pseudonymisation: fleet records require assignment to names; pseudonymisation is therefore generally not provided. Only the last four digits of fuel cards are stored.

3. Integrity (Art. 32(1)(b) GDPR)

Transfer control:

  • Connections between the apps, the web application and Google services are encrypted with TLS (HTTPS). Expo states that its connections to Apple and Google for push notifications are also encrypted via HTTPS.
  • Push notifications in the web application are sent to the browser's push service encrypted under the Web Push standard.
  • Google states that it encrypts data in Firestore and Cloud Storage at rest.

Input control:

  • Handovers are written server-side and cannot subsequently be changed or deleted by users; they contain the time and the persons involved.
  • For incidents, it is visible who corrected them.

4. Availability and resilience (Art. 32(1)(b) and (c) GDPR)

  • Operation on Google's infrastructure; Firestore in the multi-region location nam5.
  • The apps allow capture without an internet connection; the data is synchronised later, and photos and videos are kept on the device until they have been uploaded.

5. Data minimisation and deletion (Art. 25, Art. 5(1)(c) and (e) GDPR)

  • Automatic deletion of photos and videos on the 15th of the following month (15 to 45 days), not while the vehicle is still with the driver; photos and videos marked as damage on the 15th following the 90th day after they were taken.
  • Photos of fuel cards and documents are deleted right after reading; only the last four digits of the fuel card are stored.
  • Videos are stored without location information; the original video is deleted once the playback copy has been created.
  • No collection of location data, driving licence copies or numbers, date of birth, home address or health data.
  • The tours function is active only if the customer switches it on.
  • Sign-up requests that end without joining (rejected, withdrawn or expired after 14 days): mobile number, email address and Transporter ID are removed; the name and the dates of the request remain.
  • No advertising, no analytics or tracking tools, no sale of data.
  • Deletion of a user account in the app; the customer's entries bearing the person's name remain with the customer.

6. Processor control

  • Sub-processors only with contract terms under Art. 28 GDPR (see Annex 3).
  • AI: under Anthropic's Commercial Terms, Anthropic may not use content from the API to train models. According to Anthropic, inputs and outputs are generally deleted within 30 days; up to 2 years where a usage policy violation is suspected.

7. Regular review (Art. 32(1)(d) GDPR)

  • The access rules (Firestore Security Rules) and the rules for the vehicle and driver history are tested automatically on emulators before every release; if the tests fail, nothing is released.
  • Fratius reviews these measures and the list of sub-processors at least once a year and whenever the service changes materially.

8. Organisational measures

  • Currently only the owner has access to customer data (§ 3(2)).
  • Future employees will be obliged to confidentiality and instructed in data protection.
